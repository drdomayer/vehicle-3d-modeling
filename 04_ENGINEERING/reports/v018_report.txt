=== 1. FRONT FENDER TOP — the flagged +161 mm ==============================
Six parameter tests before choosing anything. Every test measured with the X-smoothing that
build() applies, because ring() alone does not predict the built surface — that is exactly how
this fault was introduced in v017.

  test                              fend350  door440  gap440  hood350  hood420
  CURRENT v017                          839      856     114      924      950
  A  X0 340 / ramp 110                  780      844     126      922      949
  B  X0 385 / ramp  80                  744      814     156      922      949
  C  X0 420 / ramp  55                  738      789     181      922      949
  D  X0 285 / ramp 155 + BELT_Z falls   763      827     143      922      949
  E  X0 320 / ramp 120 + BELT_Z falls   758      827     143      922      949
  G  X0 340 / ramp 110 + BELT_Z falls   764      841     129      922      949
  J  same, BELT_Z 345:740 420:920       764      841     129      922      949

  The two parameters move the fender and the door top together at close to 1:1 — the X-smoothing
  couples them and there is no setting that separates them. Giving BELT_Z an explicit low value
  at specX 345 is the one lever that does separate them, and it SATURATES: G and J are identical
  although their BELT_Z values differ by 15 and 8 mm. Below about 755 the shelf drops under the
  section's own profile and cabin_flank has nothing left to pull. G is therefore the frontier,
  not a preference.

  Chosen: CABIN_X0 285 -> 340, CABIN_RAMP 155 -> 110, BELT_Z gains (345, 755) and (420, 880->912),
  plus (560, 864 -> 838) to keep the belt line smooth after the front moved.

=== 2. DOOR CHANNEL — NO CHANGE MADE, AND WHY ==============================
I was told the cause was the depth table and not the measurement. It is the measurement, and the
number I reported in v017 was wrong. Reporting that rather than adjusting a field that is correct.

  The field itself, evaluated directly, through the stations in question:
    specX      505    610    715    820    925   1030
    asks     126.8  126.9  120.9  115.6  113.5  111.4       monotone, no dip anywhere

  The built surface, measured exactly on the smoothed rings that build() writes as vertices:
    400:21 505:41 610:52 715:57 820:58 925:63 1030:66 1135:69 1240:71 1345:72 1450:72 1555:72
    1660:71 1765:71 1870:67 1975:68 2080:61 2185:19
    neighbour deltas: 19 12 4 2 4 3 3 3 0 1 1 1 0 3 1 8 42
  Through specX 715..820 the deltas are 2 and 4 — the SMALLEST on the car, not the largest.

  The "80, 66, 65, 80" in the v017 report came from binning Z into 30 mm buckets before taking the
  shoulder reference. The bucket boundary lands differently at each station, which injects about
  +-15 mm of noise. Rerun on the same mesh at the same moment, the two methods disagree by 23 mm at
  specX 715 and by 9 mm at 505. The exact method needs no bins because the rings are already the
  surface.

  Two roughnesses in the channel ARE real and neither is at specX 715:
    - the run-in, deltas 19 and 12 at specX 400..610. By design; it starts from zero.
    - the release at specX 2185. Reported here as a 42 mm step and that too was a MEASUREMENT
      artifact, corrected on review. The rear arch is a 365 mm cylinder on specX 2415, so it spans
      2050..2780 and specX 2185 sits inside it: the chord loses the lower shoulder it needs to the
      arch aperture, not to any step in the surface. The field itself releases smoothly from 124 to
      0 between specX 2110 and 2300, which is the intake mouth closing before the wheel arch — a
      packaging consequence, since the arch begins at 2050 and the mouth has nowhere else to go.
      Not a blockout defect, and not evidence of anything wrong in the side channel.

=== 4. NOSE FACETING =======================================================
Diagnosed before changing anything, and the stated cause was wrong. The body is NOT short of
sections at the nose — it carries stations every 17 to 33 mm, fourteen of them across the mouth.

  The CUTTER was the facet. It was a four-corner rectangle lofted through six stations up to 80 mm
  apart: a box. So the opening had flat panels along its length and a 90 degree knife edge where it
  met the skin.

  Resampled to 12 mm through Catmull-Rom, so a coarse table is not just replaced by short facets
  joined at the same kinks, and the two INNER corners are filleted at 26 mm, capped by the
  opening's own height and depth. Same position, same depth, same height — sampling and a radius.
